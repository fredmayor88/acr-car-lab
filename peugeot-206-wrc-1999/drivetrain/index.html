How we worked it out

The reasoning and the in-game runs behind the numbers above.

Every speed on these pages is revs turned into road speed: rpm × tyre circumference ÷ total ratio, where the total ratio is primary × gear × final drive. The circumference comes from the tyre's free radius in the game files, times a rolling factor of 0.9904, fitted to the top speeds Fred measured on 14 runs across seven cars (Stratos, 306 Maxi, Xsara WRC, 037, 206 WRC, Delta Integrale and Impreza).

The rev limit, 7400 rpm, was measured in game with telemetry. The end of the torque curve in the game files is not the limiter: on every car here the curve runs past it.

The 206 has no engine curve of its own: its car data in the game files points at the Xsara WRC's, so that is the curve used here.

Its gear sets store a primary of 20//25, which is not one of the three Primary Gear steps. Either the Primary Gear you pick replaces the stored one, or 20//25 still applies. Fred ran gear set 1 on 21//24 and gear set 3 on 22//24. The speeds matched the selected primary, while 20//25 would have put them about 8% and 14% too high: the selector replaces the gear set's primary.

Next, Center Differential Ratio. Fred ran gear set 3 on 24//24, a ratio of 1, and then on 31//21 (1.476), with everything else the same. The speeds dropped by a factor of about 1.47 from one run to the other, in line with the ratio: Center Differential Ratio multiplies the whole final drive.

Last, the front and rear apart: Differential Ratio Front 43//13*21//13 and Differential Ratio Rear 40//18*26//16. The rear alone would have meant about 174 km/h in 4th, the front alone about 117. Fred got 142; the average of the two ratios puts it at 140: each axle counts half.

Why an average: between the gearbox and the axles the drivetrain splits the gearbox output to the front and the rear. With all four wheels at the same road speed, the gearbox turns at the average of what the two axles ask of it.
